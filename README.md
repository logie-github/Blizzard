# Blizzard

An Android launcher for Blizzard's games, styled after the World of Warcraft app in
`logie-github/YeaBwoi` (`warcraft/android`): its frost palette, Cinzel and Jost, the game's window
frames and the drifting snow, in Blizzard's blue. Package `com.logie.blizz`, Android 8 or later,
arm64 phones. Made for the Galaxy Z Fold.

## The look

**The look** is one:  the game's art, its name, its versions (where it has more than one) and **Play**. Everything else for a game (launch options, controls, add-ons, modules, art, install & repair, the app it plays in) is in its menu: hold its box, or tap the ••• beside Play. On the open Fold the library runs down the left; on the cover screen it is a row of boxes under the game. The gear opens the settings. **Music → Change** opens the music picker on khinsider: Blizzard Entertainment's soundtracks (its publisher page on downloads.khinsider.com) to choose an album from, a filter box, then the album's songs in a table like khinsider's (number, a ▶ to hear it, song name, length) with **Use** on each to make it the game's music. Playing a song shows a small player under the table (the album's cover, the song, the album, play or pause, how far); the launcher's own music waits while a song previews. **My files** is still there for a song on the phone. Where khinsider turns a plain request away with its browser check, the page is read in a WebView nobody sees, as a browser would. A page the app can't read anything from is sent to the developer (what it said and the start of its HTML), once a day, so the reading can be fitted to the real page. Where the game's name stands is its **logo** (the one chosen in Art, else the game's own, else its name in Blizzard's lettering); the box shows only in the library. **Music** (in the same menu) shows the track that plays for a game and lets you choose a song of your own for it, as Art does for pictures; the home always plays the music of the game it shows. **Art** (in a game's menu) holds all its pictures: its box, the **home picture** that fills the screen behind the game, and, for its loading screen, a picture and a logo; tap one to change it, its ↺ to put the original back. Under them is the loading screen as it will look: drag and pinch the picture, tap where the logo goes, slide for its size. **Cropping:** a picture chosen for the box or the home picture opens a crop first (a frame of the box's 2:3, or of the inside screen's shape for the home picture; drag to move, pinch or slide to zoom, Save keeps what the frame shows). **Crop** under either picture adjusts it again from the whole picture, which the app keeps; the built-in art can be cropped too, and **Original** puts the built-in one back. In SteamGridDB's gallery each picture has its size under it ("600 × 900", and its style). **SteamGridDB:** put your API key in Settings (Launcher page, SteamGridDB card: steamgriddb.com, sign in, your name → Preferences → API → Generate API Key; it is checked with the site before it is kept, stays in the app's own storage and goes only to steamgriddb.com). With a key, tapping a picture in Art opens that game's pictures on SteamGridDB (found by its name, the other matches as chips, a search box to change it, "My files" for a file on the phone): boxes from its grids, the home and loading pictures from its heroes, logos from its logos. Tap one and it is downloaded and set. Without a key, tapping asks for a file as before. A picture that is half or more in fire's colours (Warcraft II's orange sky over its blue sea) gets rising embers even where a cold colour is the single heaviest. The particles over the home screen and the loading screen are made from the picture's own colours: its highs and lows (the bright and the deep end of its main colour) and a few accents, rising as embers over a fiery picture (Diablo's reds) and falling as snow over an icy one; a picture with no colour gets white snow.

The classic look (the shelf, the pages, the tiles) is archived in `archive/classic-look/`: its code is kept there, with how to bring it back, and nothing in the app reaches it.

## Auto Import

Diablo, Diablo II, StarCraft and StarCraft II are imported into the app and played on its own
Winlator (Wine and Box64), offline, with the touch gestures of YeaBwoi's Warcraft app. On a game's
page, **Auto Import** opens the wizard: Detect, Acquire, Request (only what it can't get itself),
Install, Validate. Then the button becomes **Play**; the status line under it opens the wizard
Every button gives a small press (a shrink and a tick of haptic feedback); the wizard's steps and progress bars fade and glide, and when the game is ready **Play** takes the full width above Reset and Repair.
again for **Repair**.

Where it looks: the app's import folder (`Android/data/com.logie.blizz/files/import`, which a
computer can copy into over USB), every folder chosen with **Search a folder** (kept), and, after
**Search the phone**, the whole of the phone's own storage.

**Search the phone** (in a game's import, beside Search a folder, until it is allowed): one tap and Android's
question, and the importers find the game's files themselves wherever they were put, six folders deep; the
photo, music and other apps' folders (`DCIM`, `Pictures`, `Movies`, `Music`, `Android` and the like) and hidden
folders are passed over. A file that was picked on the phone's storage is then read where it is, with what is
beside it: an installer has its data files (the phone's Warcraft II from GOG, an 859 KB program with its
`.bin` next to it, was refused three times as "needs its data files": 4 and 8 November 2026). The same now
holds for an installer the search finds inside a folder chosen with Search a folder. Where Android's storage
question is not enough (the app targets Android 9 for the games' programs, which still reads all of the storage
with it on the Androids tried on paper; not seen on a phone), the app opens Android's *All files access* page
for itself, and the search runs when you come back with it switched on. A picked folder that has since been
emptied gives way to what the search finds. Nothing on the phone's storage is written; a picked *file* is
deleted after an import that checked out, as before, where Android lets the app.

**Warcraft III's CD keys** (in the game's page, **Your CD keys**): paste your Reign of Chaos and Frozen Throne keys in any form (with or without dashes and spaces, in any grouping, both at once, a label before one); each is put with its game by what it decodes to (product 14 or 18), and the box shows them shortened (`ABCD…WXYZ`). 1.27 reads its key from its own archives, so the key is written into `war3.mpq` / `War3x.mpq` as the game is installed: with a game already installed, **Save and install again** installs it again with the keys (the download again; saves kept). Keys are kept on the phone only (`files/cdkeys.txt`) and never in a report; with none given the stand-in keys are used.

**Reset and Repair, on every game.** **Reset** (in a game's menu, and in its Install & repair page) deletes the game and installs it again from the start, after a confirmation, with progress in the page; saves stay. For Warcraft III it resets both games, which share one install; for World of Warcraft: Forever it resets the realm (the client stays; *Reset everything* in its Settings page also deletes the client). **Repair** only checks the files and mends what is wrong, keeping every good one. A game whose files only you can give (Diablo, StarCraft II, Warcraft, Warcraft II) asks for them again after a Reset.

**An update that changes how a game is installed installs it again by itself** (what is downloaded, which version, what goes into its archives: each game's install carries a recipe number, and an install made by an older one is made again at the app's next start when online, or at Play, with its progress in the wizard; saves are kept, as with Reset). Build 157 does this for Warcraft III (the stand-in keys) and Diablo II (1.13c).

Statuses: Ready to Play, Imported — Launch Untested, Import Incomplete, Missing Required File,
Unsupported Client Version, Launch Test Failed. A game's first window coming up under Winlator is
recorded apart from the file checks.

What's checked: the game's program (Windows x86, its version), every DLL its import table names
(Windows' own aside), every data archive (an MPQ header), the expansion's archives. Saves are kept
apart from the game folders (`files/saves`, or inside the games' Windows) and no import or repair
writes over one. Blizzard's and Battle.net's names are blocked in the runtime's hosts file.

Version notes:

- Diablo II: 1.13 and 1.14 are accepted; the download is patched to 1.13c. Older clients are refused (they need the disc).
- StarCraft: Blizzard serves 1.15.2; installations at 1.15.2 to 1.16.1 are accepted. Blizzard no
  longer serves the 1.16.1 patch, so the app doesn't apply it. Remastered is refused.
- StarCraft II: stays **Imported — Launch Untested**: files can't show that the offline launcher
  works under Winlator or that the campaigns load.

DevilutionX is under its Sustainable Use License (non-commercial use, free distribution); the app
downloads the project's own release on the phone and redistributes nothing of it.

Diablo and Diablo II start behind their own splash (`branding/splash/`): the art fills the screen,
cut at its sides (Diablo II is never cut at its top), its logo low over it, embers rising.

## Bug reports

Crashes, failed imports and failed game starts are sent to this repository as GitHub issues by
themselves (labels `bug`, `auto-report`), with the app's version, the phone, the game, what the
app said, its checks and the end of the log; CD keys and tokens are taken out. They're queued in
`files/reports` and sent at once, again every minute while any wait, and the same failure goes
once a day at most. Only an import you stop yourself isn't sent; a full phone, an unsupported game version and a failed app-update download are.
"The same failure" is the report's title with its numbers taken out, so a report that is not a failure and has one
title (what Wrath++'s game was started with) went through once a day and said nothing of a day's changes: that one, and
a crash of Wrath++'s game read from its runtime's log, are sent each time they have something new to say (`submit`, not
the once-a-day rule; the Wrath++ section says when).

**An app update's download carries on from where it stopped.** The update (about 260 MB) is fetched in the background
when the app comes to the front; a connection that drops or times out is opened again for the rest of the file (an
HTTP range request), as long as each try brings more, and what arrived is kept for the next check under the build's
number (`cache/updates/<build>-Blizzard.apk.part`). Five tries in a row that bring nothing give up until the next check.
Before, every failure started again from nothing (reports #94 to #101: six in a row on one phone). Half a download of a
build that is no longer newer than the one running is deleted. Tested against a local server that drops the connection
(`UpdaterTest`); not on a phone.

They go with the token CI builds in, as YeaBwoi's apps carry theirs: the repository secret
`BLIZZ_REPORT_TOKEN` (a token that may open issues here). Without it, reports wait on the phone.

## Updates

Each time it opens, the app looks at this repository's releases (`blizzard-build-N`); a newer build
downloads by itself (a banner shows it) and Android's installer opens for the one tap Android
requires. The first time, Android asks to allow installs from this app.

## Testing the Windows games here (no phone)

Winlator's Wine is an x86_64 Linux build (the phone runs it through Box64), so an x86_64 Linux machine runs the very same Wine natively, against the very same game folders the downloader makes, under a virtual display, and the dialogs, logs and screenshots are read instead of guessed at. This is how Diablo II's crash was found (above). The recipe, as run on 9 November 2026 on Ubuntu 24.04 (glibc 2.39, the same as the runtime's):

1. `apt-get install zstd xvfb xdotool x11-utils imagemagick mesa-vulkan-drivers libgl1-mesa-dri`; the Wine binaries link against this host's libraries (only Wine's own `ntdll.so`/`win32u.so`, found at run time, and an ISDN library show as missing in `ldd`).
2. Unpack `app/winlator/assets/rootfs.tzst` at `/data/data/com.logie.blizz/winrootfs` (the path compiled into Wine's server), `rootfs_patches.tzst` over it; `mkdir winrootfs/tmp`. Wine is `winrootfs/opt/wine/bin/wine` (10.10).
3. A prefix: unpack `container_pattern.tzst` somewhere (its `.wine`), then copy the DLLs named in `app/winlator/assets/common_dlls.json` from `opt/wine/lib/wine/x86_64-windows` into `drive_c/windows/system32` and from `i386-windows` into `syswow64` (Winlator does this when it makes a container), plus `cmd.exe` and `start.exe`; link `dosdevices/d:` at the games folder. Never run `wineboot` (it hangs installing Mono; Winlator never runs it).
4. `Xvfb :99 -screen 0 1600x1200x24 &`; then, with `DISPLAY=:99 WINEPREFIX=<prefix>/.wine HOME=winrootfs/home/xuser WINEESYNC=1`, `cd` into the game's folder (the Unix cwd becomes its `D:\...` folder) and `wine Game.exe -w`. `WINEDEBUG=+err,+seh,+loaddll` shows what loads and what faults; `+relay` every Win32 call. `xwininfo -root -tree` lists the windows, `import -window root shot.png` takes the picture, `xdotool mousemove X Y click 1` and `xdotool type` drive it.

Winlator's `winhandler.exe` needs the Android side and does nothing useful on Xvfb; the explorer desktop is where the games run on the phone, so a game is started here as the phone starts it but for that helper: `wine explorer /desktop=shell,WxH C:\windows\borderless.exe D:\game\Game.exe args` (the keeper, below, copied into `drive_c/windows`; a second program joins with the same `/desktop=shell,WxH`, and one started with plain `wine` lands on another desktop and sees none of the game's windows). No GPU: GDI and software GL (Mesa's llvmpipe) only. Warcraft III 1.27 runs on that through Wine's own Direct3D 9 (menus, campaigns, missions; slowly, a few frames a second in a mission). **Its movies** (`Movies\*.mpq`: AVI, Blizzard's DivX and MP3, played through DirectShow) need GStreamer's plugins: without them the game faults as a movie starts (a null pointer in `Game.dll`, in a timer handler), and with every plugin of this machine Wine's process is aborted (`bit out of range 0 - FD_SETSIZE on fd_set`), so give Wine a folder of links to the same plugins the phone's runtime has (`ls winrootfs/usr/lib/gstreamer-1.0`, the ones this machine has by the same name; `apt-get install gstreamer1.0-plugins-base gstreamer1.0-plugins-good gstreamer1.0-plugins-bad gstreamer1.0-libav`) with `GST_PLUGIN_SYSTEM_PATH_1_0=<that folder> GST_PLUGIN_PATH_1_0= GST_REGISTRY_1_0=<a file> GST_REGISTRY_FORK=no`. The first start plays the intro unless `HKCU\Software\Blizzard Entertainment\Warcraft III\Misc\seenintromovie` is 1.

**Winlator's own X server** (pointer grabs, the stretch, which window gets a click: where the phone's input differs from Xvfb) runs on a desktop too, in a unit test with the same Wine: `tools/xrig/README.md`. A fault in a game's clicks or keys is reproduced there. The phone's own Box64 runs there as well (arm64, under `qemu-user`), and the rig can show the screen as the phone's renderer composes it: the same file, "Through the phone's Box64".

**The Forever client and realm on the rig.** The client: `ForeverClientInstaller` at priority 0 (782 MB, the scratch runner in the session notes) into `<games>/forever/client`, `dosdevices/f:` linked at `<games>/forever`, `wine explorer /desktop=shell,1600x1200 F:\client\_forever_\Wow.exe` from the `_forever_` folder; it streams the rest it needs from Blizzard's CDN (and keeps downloading in the background: watch the disk). For the phone's Direct3D, unpack `dxwrapper/dxvk-2.4.1.tzst` and `vkd3d-2.14.1.tzst` into the prefix's `windows` and set `WINEDLLOVERRIDES=d3d8,d3d9,d3d10core,d3d11,d3d12,d3d12core,dxgi=n,b` (Winlator writes the same as registry overrides); the client picks Direct3D 12 (vkd3d-proton) and runs on Mesa's lavapipe. The game writes its logs (`_forever_/Logs`) when it quits, not when it is killed: leave by its Quit button to read `Connection.log`. The realm: `apt-get install qemu-user-static`, mount `binfmt_misc` and register `qemu-aarch64-static` (flag `F`), unpack the `forever-server` release's package at `/data/data/com.logie.blizz/files/forever` (the path it is built for) and drive it with `mksh server/ctl.sh` exactly as the app does (`configure`, `db`, `realm-build 70862`, `bnetserver`, `worldserver` with a FIFO on its console for `bnetaccount create`). Map data without the 47 GB client: `mapextractor -c 1 -r us` reads Blizzard's CDN, which it reaches over plain HTTP; where only proxied HTTPS goes out, a small HTTP-to-HTTPS forwarder on ports 80 and 1119 with the CDN's names in `/etc/hosts` serves it (bnetserver stopped meanwhile: it owns 1119). It caches whole archives: Eastern Kingdoms and Kalimdor took 6.6 GB of cache before the rest was stopped. Add `127.0.0.1 trinity.actual.battle.net` to `/etc/hosts`, copy `ca/ca.pem` to the prefix's two `web_cert_bundle` places as the app does, and keep `trinity.actual.battle.net` out of any proxy (`no_proxy`): the game's HTTPS login otherwise goes to the proxy and fails (`ERROR_HTTP_FAILED 14003`).

**borderless.exe, the session's keeper** (`tools/borderless/borderless.c`, built by `scripts/build-tools.sh` with Zig for x86_64 Windows into the app's assets). The app copies it into the games' Windows (`C:\windows`) at every start, and the game screen starts it first (Winlator patch 0009, the `exec_wrapper` extra): `explorer /desktop=shell,WxH C:\windows\borderless.exe [/noframes] C:\windows\winhandler.exe /dir D:\game "Game.exe" args`. It starts the rest of its command line and, for as long as the game runs, does three things nothing else in the games' Windows does:

1. **The games' desktop takes the mouse.** `SwitchDesktop` to its own desktop at the start and every tick, so input that Wine reports without a window (a locked cursor's: Warcraft III) goes to the games and not to the window station's first desktop.
2. **No title bars.** The games' Windows is Wine's virtual desktop, where Wine itself draws every window's title bar and frame (no window manager on Winlator's X server) and has no setting against it, so a game started in a window (Warcraft III 1.2x's `-window`, Diablo II's `-w`) sat under its own bar, pushed down and cut at the bottom. It takes the caption, frame and box styles off each visible top-level window of the games (not Wine's own programs: explorer, the taskbar; not dialog boxes) and puts it at the top left: a window that can be sized gets the whole screen (Warcraft III), one of a fixed size keeps its client area's size (the screen's, when it filled the screen's width: Diablo II's 806x612 with a client area of 800x587 becomes 800x600) and the launcher stretches it. It keeps watching, for a window a game makes again (Warcraft III after each movie); a frameless window that hangs a little over the screen's edge at the top left (Diablo II as a game begins) is trimmed to the screen. Not with `/noframes` (the World of Warcraft clients, an installer). Wine refuses to open the game's process for its name (access denied on a 32-bit one): a window whose program can't be named counts as the game's.
3. **The session ends with the game.** `winhandler.exe` stays for as long as the desktop does and the desktop for as long as it: a game quit from its own menu, or crashed, left a black screen until the app's menu was found. When no program of the game's is left for three seconds (or none ever started in a minute and a half), it ends `winhandler.exe` and exits; the desktop closes, Wine exits, and the app is back at the launcher. A launcher program that starts the game and leaves (Warcraft III's `Frozen Throne.exe`) does not end it.

It keeps the file `C:\windows\borderless.dialog` there for as long as a dialog box of the game's is up (Windows' own kind, class `#32770`: Winlator's X server cannot tell one from a game's window, Wine sets no dialog type on it in its desktop), which is how the app knows to make a strategy game's tap the left click then. It writes what it did and saw to `C:\windows\borderless.log` (started anew each session): the desktop switch, what it started, each of the game's windows as it appears (class, title, size, place) and as it goes while the game runs, a dialog box's words (the game's own error message), each frame taken off, the game's programs coming and going, why it ended. It never waits on a window whose program is busy (a change of style is a message to the window's own thread: Warcraft III's is busy for the whole of a movie, and the keeper stood still for the intro's 90 seconds until it asked first). The app puts that into every report. Verified on the desktop: on Winlator's own X server with the probe and Diablo II 1.13 (`tools/xrig/README.md`), and on Xvfb with Warcraft III 1.27 (592x423 with a bar to 800x600 without, again after each movie) and a dialog (`winver`: its title and texts in the log, the session over three seconds after OK).

## Controls

Each game's **Launch options** (in its menu) has, besides Safe mode, DirectDraw, frames, Box64 and the rest, a **Command line** box: switches of your own, added after the app's at the game's start (Diablo II's `-ns`, `-3dfx`, `-ddraw`, `-d3d`; Warcraft III's `-opengl`), for trying a fix without a new build.

Each game's **Controls** button (beside Play) lists every function with its key and the gestures that run it, and the gesture rules: *If [gesture] [where] then [function]*. Keys are the game's own defaults (Diablo's are DevilutionX's); change a key by tapping it. Add a rule with the record button: do the gesture on the pad and it names the gesture and where it was; pick what it does. Where several rules match, the smallest area wins; "Nothing" switches a spot off. Saved per game; the reset button restores the defaults.


The one gesture system (`controls/Gestures.kt`), two profiles (`game/LaunchProfile.kt`):

| | StarCraft, StarCraft II | Diablo, Diablo II |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tap | Right click: move, attack, gather. On the game's panels and bars (StarCraft's console), where a right click does nothing, a left click | Left click: walk, attack, pick up |
| Touch and hold | Left click when you lift: select a unit or building, press a menu button (hold twice on a spot: double click) | Right click: cast |
| Swipe | Pan the map | Drag: walk after the finger. Quick flick: the arrow key its way (menus) |
| Resting finger, then tap / hold | Shift + right click (queue an order) / Shift + left click (add to the selection) | Shift |
| Two / three / four fingers | Game menu / keyboard / app menu | Same |

StarCraft keeps this layout. As its window comes up, one line says so: *Hold to select or press a button · tap to move or attack*. That line is gone before the game has drawn its menu, where a tap (the right click) presses nothing, so until the first hold of the session a tap says it again (*Touch and hold to press a button or select · a tap is the right click*), up to four times. The game's own menus are drawn inside its window and cannot be told from the battlefield; a dialog box of Windows' own can (an error message, a prompt: the session's keeper says when one is up), and there a tap is the left click.

**Warcraft III had its own layout on Winlator** (it plays in Warsmash now, with the engine's own touch: **Warcraft III (Warsmash)**; yours, 10 November 2026: "Tap to select then tap to move. Double tap for left click. Hold tap for right click."):

| Gesture | Warcraft III |
| --- | --- |
| Tap | The smart tap. With nothing of yours selected it is the left click: it selects the unit or building you tap, presses any menu's button, and gives a target or a place to build when the game asks for one. With your units or a building selected it is the right click: move there, attack that, gather, set the rally point |
| Double tap | Always the left click: select another unit while some are selected |
| Tap, then tap and hold | The game's double click: every unit of that kind on the screen |
| Touch and hold | Always the right click (when you lift, after the buzz) |
| Hold, then drag | The selection box |
| Drag | Scroll the map. On the minimap it is the game's own drag: the view follows the finger |

On the game's own buttons (the bottom panel, the top bar, the hero icons, the idle worker's icon) a tap is always the left click, and two taps press twice. **How the tap knows:** the game shuts its process to other programs, so the app reads the game's own picture, a few times a second and as each finger lands: the gold coin of the resource bar says a mission's interface is up, the Menu button's words are lit only while the game takes orders (they go grey under the game's menu, the quest log, a defeat box), and the command card bottom right has buttons only for your own units and buildings, and only Cancel while the game waits for a target or a place. Where the picture cannot be read, or shows anything else (a menu, a movie, a loading screen, a box over the game), a tap is the left click: every button can always be pressed, and the hold still orders. The two-finger tap goes by the same look (Escape where the picture shows no mission taking orders, or a command waiting for its target; while a box of Windows' own is up; the game's menu otherwise, and whenever the picture cannot be read). With units selected a tap waits a quarter of a second for a second one (the double tap), so an order follows the finger by that much; everything else acts at once. **Tested on the desktop:** the reader's rules against 78 of the game's own frames at the phone's 960x720 and at 800x600 (four races with nothing, a unit, several, a building selected; a neutral building; the game's menu, log and defeat box over them; a target wanted, a place to build wanted; the game's own cursor resting on an empty button, on the Menu button and on the coin; menus, movies, loading and score screens), each also upside down and with red and blue swapped, as the phone's graphics may hand them over; and the whole layout against the running game on the rig, deciding each tap from a screenshot as the phone does: a worker selected, moved by a tap, Attack then a tap for its target, Build then a farm placed by a tap (the gold went from 500 to 420), the game's menu opened and left by taps, then opened by the two-finger tap's F10 and left by its Escape, and an Attack called off by its Escape with the selection kept. ## Launch options

Each game that runs in Windows (not Warcraft I and II, which are native) has its own **Launch options**, to fix a black screen, missing sound or videos, or a slow game on your phone without waiting for an update: hold the game's tile (or open the game's page) and choose Launch options. A choice is kept and used at the game's next start; **Reset** puts the game's choices back.

- **Safe mode:** the slowest, safest of everything at once (cnc-ddraw's software GDI drawing, Box64 Stability, 30 frames a second, the default graphics driver and Direct3D layer); the other choices are ignored while it is on. Try it first when a game shows nothing.
- **DirectDraw** (the classic games): Wine's own, cnc-ddraw GDI, OpenGL or Direct3D 9. **Frames a second** (30, 60, no limit), **vsync**, **Keep redrawing** (cnc-ddraw's `nonexclusive` and `minfps`, for videos, menus and loading screens that stay invisible) and **Pin to one CPU core** (`singlecpu`).
- **Box64:** Stability, Compatible, Intermediate or Performance (Winlator's presets).
- The **graphics driver**, the **Direct3D layer** and the **resolution** have no setting: the app picks them (Turnip on an Adreno GPU such as the Fold's, Vortek elsewhere; DXVK; always the Fold's inside screen, its shape and its lines, whichever screen Play is pressed on: on the cover screen the game starts at the inside screen's shape between black bars, so opening the phone changes nothing. The inside screen's size is learned whenever the launcher is on it (a Fold 8's 2520x2340 until then). On an Adreno GPU, DXVK tells the games they have an AMD Radeon RX 6800 XT, the same card at every start, so their own checks pick their best settings and give no hardware or driver warnings (safe mode reports the phone's own GPU). The drivers are Winlator's newest: Turnip 26.2.0 and DXVK 2.4.1. Every game is drawn at 60 frames a second by default; nothing is lowered for heat.
- **Defaults** (Diablo II, StarCraft, Warcraft II): cnc-ddraw with OpenGL, borderless fullscreen, 60 frames, redrawing on, **no** CPU pinning (the first release pinned the game to the phone's CPU0, a slow core, which fits the 3 frames a second and the sound that never started), Box64 Intermediate. Other games keep Winlator's defaults (Performance, Vortek, DXVK).
- The games share one Windows, so every option is written at every start; changing the DirectDraw choice, the Box64 preset, the driver or the Direct3D layer also makes Winlator put its files in again. Diablo II has no switch to skip its videos, so there is none here. Untested on a phone.

## Game log

While a game runs, the app keeps the last 400 lines Wine printed (errors and exception traces; a longjmp's trace, which Diablo II makes every frame, is left out), the last 80 libraries it loaded, the session keeper's log (the game's windows, a dialog's words, why the session ended) and its own notes (each click). The game menu (four fingers) has **Send a report (with the game's log)**, which files it as a GitHub issue, for black screens that leave no crash behind. A report keeps the last 20,000 characters, and the log is ordered so that the least telling part is what gets cut: the libraries first, then the oldest of Wine's lines; the keeper's log and the app's notes are the end of it (the phone's reports of 9 and 10 November 2026 were four fifths library names, with the app's notes cut off the top).

- **Back** opens the app's menu (so does a four-finger tap).
- Every click of the touch controls is noted in the log (`[app] LEFT click at X 483,312: under 0x... 'Warcraft III' war3.exe 960x720@0,0 takes presses; grab ...; focus ...`): which button, where the X server's pointer was, the window under it (its name, class, size and place, whether it is a dialog, input-only, or takes button presses), a pointer grab if a program holds one, and the focused window. A click that does nothing in a game is read off the report, not guessed at.
- A game that shows no window for 90 seconds, and every game one minute in, send their Wine log as an issue (once a day per game), so a black screen is reported without anyone reaching the menu.
- **Repack installers** (Setup.exe with `Data01.dxn`-style files: ISDone, unarc, xtool, lolz) make the game's files only when they run. The import says so and offers **Run the installer**: the installer and its data files are copied into the app (about the game's size again is needed free, twice over with the install), run silently in the app's Windows (`/SILENT ... /DIR=T:\<game>-installer\out`), and when it has finished, **Import what it installed** (Retry until then) imports it and deletes the copy. Pick the **folder** that holds Setup.exe and its data files: a single Setup.exe picked as a file has no neighbours the app can see, so it asks for the folder. The copy shows bytes, goes on from where it stopped (files already copied are kept), and can be removed ("Remove the installer's copy", or Reset). While the installer runs the wizard says so and looks again when you come back; a game that has stopped changing for 3 minutes counts as finished, in `out` or, when the installer ignored `/DIR`, in the app's Windows C: drive (Program Files, Blizzard Entertainment, users), whence it is moved into `out`. If the installer ends having written nothing, the wizard says so with Wine's last output, and **Run the installer** again tries the next set of switches (`/SILENT`, `/VERYSILENT`, `/SP-`, `/NOCANCEL`, `/LANG=english`, no `/DIR`). Under emulation this can take hours. Untested on a phone.

## World of Warcraft (from the WoW app, nothing rebuilt)

The shelf's **World of Warcraft** box (placeholder art until you send yours) brings over what the World of Warcraft app (`com.logie.warcraft`) holds: its client, realm databases with their characters, add-ons, modules, settings and the other environments' data. In the WoW app tap **Export everything to Blizzard**, choose where to save, and it writes **one zip file** (`WoW-export.zip`: `files/`, `prefs/`, `checksums.tsv`, and `export.json` as its last entries). In Blizzard, tap the World of Warcraft box, **Auto Import**, and pick that zip. (An export folder made by older versions of the WoW app is still read.) Nothing is renamed inside a zip, so capital-letter twins arrive as they were.
- It's a **copy**: the WoW app and its data are untouched. Every file is checked against the export's SHA-256 list while it's copied, and the finished copy replaces nothing until it's whole. A file that doesn't match is named and nothing is installed.
- A stopped or failed import is **carried on**: files already copied and checked are kept in `staging/wow-work` with a journal; Retry continues. Reset removes it.
- The copy keeps the export's manifest and settings (`.export/`), including the paths the data was made under, for the next step: running the realms here. **Play starts the realm and the game here** (the WoW app isn't needed).

It takes the Windows / x86_64 / English (US, text and speech) files without the optional Alternate, HighRes and Talebound packs: **about 47 GB** for build 70862, so the phone needs a little more than that free (it says how much if not).   - A start that fails says why by itself: when the launcher comes back to the front it sends the game's own crash file (`_forever_/Errors`), or the failures in its storage log, as a report. **Auto Import** (the page's wizard) does two things:

1. **Downloads the module first:** D2R Mobile's newest release, from GitHub, of which Blizzard keeps the native library, code and assets (in `modules/d2r`); the release's APK itself is never installed.
2. **Imports your Switch files**, which must be your own Diablo II: Resurrected with Reign of the Warlock, updated to 1.0.34.0 (PC 3.3.94012): pick the **base game** (the .nsp, a folder with it, or the 7z, zip or tar that holds it), the **update** (the same, or skip when it is in the same package) and your **prod.keys**. The app unpacks them with hactool the way the project's own `extract_game.sh` does: title keys from the tickets in the .nsp files (a personalized ticket, tied to the console it came from, holds none: put that title's key in the keys file), the base game's program and the update's told apart by what hactool reports, the update extracted over the base, the three ExeFS files checked against the 3.3.94012 ones (SHA-256), and only what the game reads kept (the 18 resource archives, `.build.config`, the manifest, ExeFS: about 5.6 GB). It needs about 26 GB free while it works. The keys are copied to a private folder for the unpacking and deleted with it; nothing is uploaded.

**Not done yet:** playing it. The port runs from an *import pack* its tool makes from those files (the game's program rewritten for Android, about 6,900 translated shader bundles, the touch overlay and some tables). That tool needs Python with several packages, the .NET SDK and the Vulkan SDK's shader tools, which a phone doesn't have, so for now Play only says the files are imported. Hosting the library inside the app is feasible (a stub of the class its native code binds to, `System.load`, and a surface and touch Activity: about 1,600 lines of the project's MIT Java source adapted), but needs the pack. Untested on a phone.

## When an install fails

A failed import (the first failure of its kind each day) is sent to this repository as an issue, with what the app knows: the problem, the checks that failed, the log, and **a listing of the files involved** (what you picked, the installed folder and what the import left in staging): each file's path, size and a hash of its size and its first and last 64 KB, never its content, so the files themselves needn't be sent. Sending needs the `BLIZZ_REPORT_TOKEN` secret built into the release (see Launch test).

## Character Porter (World of Warcraft)

Find any character on Blizzard's Armory, see them in 3D as they are dressed now, and bring them into one of the phone's realms. Hold a World of Warcraft box for **Character Porter**, or open it from Settings, Games, Wrath+ (or Legion, OctoWoW): **Character Porter**.

- **Search**: pick the Armory's region (Americas & Oceania, Europe, Korea, Taiwan), type a name: each character of that name with their level, race, class, realm and faction (a hundred at a time, *More* for the next). The Armory lists characters who have played lately, above level 10, on Blizzard's own realms.
- **The character**: in 3D (drag to turn, up and down to look from above or below, pinch to come closer, double tap to stand back), beside what they wear (each item with its item level and the look it was given), their professions, how many mounts they have and how many factions have a standing with them.
- **How the 3D look is made**: from the game's own tables and files, fetched from **wago.tools** on the phone (nothing of the game is in the app): about twenty small requests for the rows a character needs (their race's model and default customizations, the layers their skin is painted from, what each worn item shows: the textures on the body, the sleeves and skirts, helms, shoulders, weapons, cloaks and the armour pieces that move with the body), then the models and textures themselves. The first character of a race downloads tens of MB (its model and skeleton); the rest come from the phone. Everything is kept under the app's `files/porter` for the game's live build; when wago.tools names a newer build, the next character fetches anew and the older build's files are deleted (a model and its skin from two builds don't fit together).
- **What the Armory's website keeps back**: the character's face, hair and skin choices (without more, the race's defaults are shown), their quests and their achievements one by one. **A Battle.net API key of your own** adds them: the key button at the top of the porter. Make it for free at develop.battle.net (sign in, *API Access*, *Create Client*: any name, no redirect address needed) and copy its Client ID and Client Secret into the porter; they are tried with Battle.net (a token is asked for) before they are kept, and they stay in the app's own storage. With it the 3D look uses their own choices and what each slot shows (Blizzard's `appearance`), and the character view counts their completed quests and achievements (`quests/completed`, `achievements`), which the port will carry over where the realm has them. The API's answers are read in the shapes a published client's types give them (`BattleNetTest`); **not tried against the API itself** (it needs a key: `BLIZZ_BNET_ID`/`BLIZZ_BNET_SECRET` run `BattleNetTest.theLiveApi`).
- **Port** (the bottom of a character's page): the phone's realms, each saying whether it can take them now. **ChromieCraft** and **Wrath++** (Wrath+'s realms; each must have been played once here, which sets it up) take a ported character; **Wrath Remix** makes its own (everyone there begins as a Timerunner); **Legion** and **OctoWoW** come in a later update (their realms keep characters in other tables). Choosing a realm: the porter reads what the Armory adds (mounts, standings) and, with a Battle.net key, the quests completed, achievements, titles and looks; stops the realm if it runs (its characters are saved first) and starts its database alone; then asks:
  - **Name**: theirs, checked as you type against the realm's name rules, its reserved names and its characters.
  - **Race, class and sex**: their own where the realm has the pair; otherwise the nearest it has, with the reason (a Void Elf is the realm's Void Elf where the allied races are in, else a Night Elf; a Monk, Demon Hunter or Evoker a Wrath class by what they do in a group). Any race and class pair of the realm can be chosen.
  - **Level**: the retail level as the same share of the way to the realm's cap (a retail level-cap character is at the cap; ChromieCraft's own progression cap, 19 as the realm starts, is the ceiling suggested; anything up to the realm's highest level can be chosen). A death knight begins at 55.
  - **Gold**: the Armory doesn't show a character's gold: choose what they bring.
  - **Gear**: *Fresh* (dungeon blues), *Raid* (early raid epics) or *Best* (the last raid tier's): each slot gets the realm's own item nearest what they wear, for their class, level and specialization.
  - **Account**: the realm account the character goes on (the one World of Warcraft logs in with comes first).
  - **See the port**: everything that will be written and why, part by part (kept, changed, added, left out) and slot by slot for the gear; nothing is written before **Port**. The port is one transaction in the realm's characters database: it goes in whole or not at all. The realm starts again with Play.
- **Looks**: Blizzard's API gives today's customization choices; the old character creator's numbers (skin colour, face, hair style, hair colour, facial hair) are found with the game's own table between the two (ChrCustomizationConversion, which Blizzard made when every character moved to the new creator, read backwards; `LegacyLooksTest` checks it over 13 races on wago.tools). A look made since has no old number: the realm's first is used, and the port says so.
- **What a port writes** (the realm's engine, `porter/port`, which reads the realm's own tables and client data and writes nothing before **Port**): the character, in its side's capital with its hearthstone bound there (murlocs and naga at their own story's start), with its race, sex, class, level, looks and gold. **Gear**, slot by slot: the very item if the realm has it and the character can wear it, else the item whose look it wears (transmogrification), else the best item the realm gives that class at that level in some way (a drop, a vendor, a quest it could have done, a recipe) for its specialization and the tier chosen; never two of one ring or trinket; the tabard only as itself or its look. With it four bags sized to the level, a hearthstone, ammunition or totems where the class needs them. **Spells**: all its class, weapon and riding trainers teach at that level and those of its class's quests (talents are left to spend). **Professions**: two primaries at most and the secondaries, their Classic, Outland and Northrend points up to what the level allows, with the trainer's recipes to that skill (Archaeology and newer are left out). **Mounts** the realm has (by their spell), with one that runs on the ground added if none of theirs does. **Standings** with the realm's factions (Renown has no counterpart). **Quests** of Wrath's own whose title is the same, **achievements** and **titles** the realm has, the map explored and the flight paths of a character of that level, and a new character's action bars. Not carried: gems, enchants, glyphs, hunter pets, guild, mail, bank, currencies. The realm's world server must be stopped meanwhile (it keeps the next ids in memory): the porter stops it and starts only the database.
- **Tested here**: the Armory reader on the live site (US and EU); the look on the game's own tables and on wago.tools itself (the same as the desktop render that was checked against the Armory's own pictures of seven characters of seven races); the app's whole path for one character, from wago.tools to a picture drawn by the same C++ viewer the app uses, on a desktop's OpenGL ES (`PreviewRenderTest`). **The port**, on the phone's own ChromieCraft server package run on this machine (its arm64 programs under emulation through its own `ctl.sh`, MariaDB of its own version: `tools/porter-rig`): six made-up characters in the Armory's shape (a human Protection warrior at 80, a blood elf Frost mage at 70, a dwarf hunter at 60, a tauren druid of 20 scaled to 4, a void elf Demon Hunter made a rogue at 19, a nightborne death knight at 80) were planned and written by the engine (`PorterRigTest`), then each logged into the world with a test client and kept every item, spell, skill, reputation, quest, achievement and action button written (`prove.sh`: the realm's own checks at login delete what they refuse, and a control run shows the check catches that); a port the database refuses midway leaves nothing behind. The Port screens on a stand-in realm (`PorterUiTest`, both screen sizes); the looks' conversion on the game's own table (`LegacyLooksTest`). **Not tested**: anything on a phone (the 3D view, the realm's database started by the porter there); a real game client (the ported character's looks, icons and character screen are unseen) and play after logging in; Wrath++ (its server isn't on the rig); a port of a real Armory character; Blizzard's API itself (it needs a key).

## Launch test

From the settings' **Run the launch test now** (it no longer runs by itself), every game is checked and each installed one is started: **is it installed** (its import record and files: where an import stopped and why), **did it finish installing**, **does it launch** (the game's first window comes up and stays for ten seconds; Warcraft I and II: Stratagus starts and stays up) and **does it run OK**. Each game is then closed. Games that were never imported are noted, not started. World of Warcraft starts its realm first (minutes the first time) and stops it afterwards. The result is a report: a table of every game, the failures with their notes and the end of the Wine log, saved on the phone, shown to copy, and sent to this repository as an issue labelled `launch-test`. If the app itself dies during a game, the next start sends what was done and names the game it died on. Sending needs the `BLIZZ_REPORT_TOKEN` secret built into the release; without it the dialog says so and the report can be copied. Untested on a phone.
